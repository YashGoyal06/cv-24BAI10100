## 2. Introduction
Automotive safety research has increasingly focused on human factors as primary catalysts in motor vehicle accidents. The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that driver fatigue, micro-sleep episodes, and visual inattention contribute to approximately 1.35 million traffic fatalities each year globally. Traditional vehicular telematics systems—such as lane departure warning systems (LDWS) or erratic steering torque sensors—operate reactively, alerting the driver only after the vehicle trajectory has already begun to drift dangerously.

In contrast, in-cabin **Driver Monitoring Systems (DMS)** utilizing Computer Vision operate proactively. By observing facial geometry and ocular micro-movements, a camera-based system can detect cognitive lapses seconds prior to physical steering deviation. **VisionGuard** is an end-to-end, real-time Computer Vision system designed to analyze live video feeds, extract dense facial landmarks, compute scale-invariant ocular and oral metrics, estimate 3D head orientation via classical Perspective-n-Point geometry, and execute multi-cue risk synthesis to issue graded, debounced safety alerts.

---

## 3. Problem Statement
Commercial long-haul transport drivers, shift workers, and daily commuters are routinely subjected to circadian rhythm disruption and cognitive fatigue. While wearable biometric devices (e.g., smartwatches measuring pulse rate or EEG headbands) provide physiological signals, they are intrusive, prone to displacement, and rarely adopted voluntarily. A non-intrusive, camera-based solution is required that:
1. Operates on consumer-grade camera hardware without expensive specialized depth sensors.
2. Formulates explainable geometric measures rather than opaque, uninterpretable predictions.
3. Suppresses false alarms generated by natural speech, smiling, or normal blinking through temporal filtering.
4. Operates in real time ($\ge 25$ frames per second) with low CPU utilization.

## 4. Objectives
The primary technical and academic objectives of VisionGuard are:
* **Objective 1:** Implement reliable facial landmark extraction using Google's MediaPipe FaceMesh to regressed 468 3D facial vertices in real time.
* **Objective 2:** Formulate mathematically grounded **Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR)** based on Soukupová and Čech (2016) to reliably distinguish instantaneous blinks from sustained micro-sleep closures ($>0.5$ seconds).
* **Objective 3:** Implement **Mouth Aspect Ratio (MAR)** with temporal persistence filtering to capture involuntary yawning episodes while rejecting transient speech and smiling.
* **Objective 4:** Estimate 3D head orientation (Pitch, Yaw, Roll) by solving the **Perspective-n-Point (SolvePnP)** problem between canonical 3D facial model points and 2D landmark projections to detect visual distraction.
* **Objective 5:** Construct a modular **Risk Assessment Engine** fusing ocular, oral, and spatial orientation cues with exponential moving average (EMA) temporal smoothing.
* **Objective 6:** Build a modern, glassmorphic **Streamlit dashboard** equipped with real-time Heads-Up Display (HUD) overlays, rate-limited auditory warnings, and exportable session telemetry (CSV/JSON).

## 7. System Architecture
VisionGuard adopts a clean, layered pipeline architecture comprising five distinct tiers:
1. **Video Acquisition Layer:** Interfaces with OpenCV `VideoCapture` to grab raw BGR camera frames.
2. **Perception Layer:** Executes MediaPipe FaceMesh to obtain 468 normalized 3D vertices and maps them to pixel coordinates.
3. **Feature Extraction Layer:** Executes independent geometric algorithms (EAR for eyes, MAR for mouth, SolvePnP for head pose).
4. **Assessment & Alert Layer:** Synthesizes extracted features using weighted linear combination, applies exponential moving average smoothing, categorizes safety state, and dispatches debounced audio alarms.
5. **Presentation Layer:** Renders the glassmorphic HUD on the video stream, updates Streamlit dashboard widgets, and handles file exports.

## 9. Computer Vision Methodology

### 9.1 Image Acquisition & Pre-processing
Video frames are acquired via OpenCV at $640 \times 480$ resolution. OpenCV natively captures frames in BGR format; these are transformed to RGB prior to neural landmark processing:
$$\mathbf{I}_{\text{RGB}} = \mathcal{T}_{\text{BGR}\to\text{RGB}}(\mathbf{I}_{\text{BGR}})$$

### 9.2 Dense 3D Facial Landmark Regression
Rather than relying on older 68-point Dlib models that struggle with extreme rotations, VisionGuard employs Google's MediaPipe FaceMesh model. FaceMesh leverages a deep 3D residual neural network operating on an anchor-based face bounding box identified by BlazeFace, outputting 468 vertices $(x_i, y_i, z_i) \in [0, 1]^3$.

### 9.3 Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR)
Defined by Soukupová and Čech (2016), EAR provides an aspect measurement invariant to scale and distance:
$$\text{EAR} = \frac{\|p_2 - p_6\|_2 + \|p_3 - p_5\|_2}{2 \cdot \|p_1 - p_4\|_2}$$
Where $p_1, p_4$ are the lateral eye corners, and $(p_2, p_6), (p_3, p_5)$ are vertical eyelid landmark pairs.

### 9.4 Mouth Aspect Ratio (MAR)
Oral aperture is quantified relative to oral width:
$$\text{MAR} = \frac{\|p_{\text{top}} - p_{\text{bottom}}\|_2 + \|p_{\text{mid\_top}} - p_{\text{mid\_bottom}}\|_2}{2 \cdot \|p_{\text{left}} - p_{\text{right}}\|_2}$$

### 9.5 3D Perspective-n-Point (SolvePnP) Pose Estimation
To calculate head orientation, VisionGuard establishes a correspondence between 6 anthropometric 3D canonical facial points (Nose tip, Chin, Eye corners, Mouth corners) and their respective 2D pixel coordinates regressed by MediaPipe. Given the camera intrinsic calibration matrix $\mathbf{K}$, the transformation is solved via:
$$s \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{K} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R} & \mathbf{t} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} X_w \\ Y_w \\ Z_w \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
The resulting rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ is decomposed into Tait-Bryan Euler angles:
* **Pitch ($\theta_x$):** Vertical head nod.
* **Yaw ($\theta_y$):** Horizontal head turn (driver looking out side window).
* **Roll ($\theta_z$):** Lateral head tilt.

## 11. Model & Algorithm Selection Rationale

| Alternative Considered | Selected Technique | Selection Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Dlib 68 Landmark Shape Predictor | MediaPipe FaceMesh (468 points) | Dlib requires heavy compilation, exhibits slower frame rates on standard laptops, and fails when head turns $>25^\circ$. MediaPipe is lightweight, highly optimized, and includes iris refinement. |
| End-to-End CNN / RNN on Raw Video | Geometric EAR + MAR + SolvePnP | End-to-end neural networks function as unexplainable black boxes, require GPU hardware for real-time inference, and suffer from domain shifts across different camera angles. Geometric metrics are mathematically explainable and easily calibrated. |
| Single-Frame Thresholding | Multi-Frame Temporal Windowing | Instantaneous thresholding causes high false alarms from single blinks or speech syllables. Temporal persistence guarantees deliberate events. |
| Raw Instantaneous Score | Exponential Moving Average (EMA) | Smooths high-frequency sensor noise while preserving rapid escalation during genuine hazards. |

## 13. Risk Assessment Logic
The risk engine computes an instantaneous hazard index $\mathcal{H}_t \in [0, 100]$:
$$\mathcal{H}_t = \left( 0.40 \cdot f_{\text{eye}} + 0.25 \cdot f_{\text{yawn}} + 0.25 \cdot f_{\text{dist}} + 0.10 \cdot f_{\text{blink}} \right) \times 100$$
Temporal smoothing is applied via EMA:
$$\mathcal{R}_t = 0.25 \cdot \mathcal{H}_t + 0.75 \cdot \mathcal{R}_{t-1}$$

The final safety state is determined hierarchically:
* `NO FACE`: Face temporarily lost or occluded.
* `HIGH RISK`: Prolonged eye closure coinciding with head turned away, or $\mathcal{R}_t \ge 80$.
* `DROWSY`: Prolonged eye closure $\ge 15$ frames ($\approx 0.5$s), or $\mathcal{R}_t \ge 60$.
* `DISTRACTED`: Head yaw $|\theta_y| > 25^\circ$ for $\ge 20$ frames ($\approx 0.67$s).
* `CAUTION`: Sustained yawning confirmed, or $\mathcal{R}_t \ge 30$.
* `SAFE`: Nominal biometrics within normal operating limits.

## 18. Design Decisions & Rationale
1. **Decision to use MediaPipe FaceMesh over Dlib:** Eliminates external C++ CMake build requirements and provides 468 3D landmarks at twice the framerate of Dlib.
2. **Decision to use SolvePnP for Head Pose:** Provides true 3D spatial rotation (Pitch, Yaw, Roll) using projective pinhole geometry rather than crude 2D landmark heuristic ratios.
3. **Decision to Implement Debounced Auditory Cues:** Continuous alarm beeping creates driver irritation. A 2.0-second cooldown ensures audible warnings remain effective without becoming disruptive.
4. **Decision to Decouple Configuration into Dataclasses:** Centralizes all biometric thresholds into `src/config.py`, making the codebase clean, maintainable, and free of magic numbers.

## 19. Challenges Faced & Solutions

| Challenge Encountered | Root Cause | Solution Implemented |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **False Yawn Alarms During Speech** | Natural pronunciation of vowels ('O', 'Ah') briefly opens the mouth | Implemented a 15-frame continuous persistence buffer and a 60-frame cooldown timer. |
| **Blink vs. Micro-Sleep Confusion** | Eyelids close completely during both events | Separated single blinks ($1-4$ frames) from prolonged micro-sleep closures ($\ge 15$ frames). |
| **Audio Stuttering Freezing UI** | Synchronous sound playback halts video capture | Offloaded audio playback to detached background worker threads. |
| **Landmark Jitter in Low Light** | Sensor noise causing minor coordinate fluctuations | Applied Exponential Moving Average (EMA) smoothing to the composite risk score. |

## 20. Limitations
* **Visible Spectrum Lighting:** Performance depends on ambient illumination; pitch-black vehicle cabins require an active Near-Infrared (NIR) camera.
* **Extreme Angles:** Turning the head beyond $\pm 45^\circ$ causes occlusion of the opposite eye, which may temporarily reduce EAR precision.
* **Eyewear:** Highly reflective sunglasses can block optical visibility of the pupils.

## 21. Learnings & Key Takeaways
* Geometric feature engineering (EAR, MAR, SolvePnP) provides reliable, explainable, and resource-efficient results compared to black-box neural networks for real-time video edge applications.
* Temporal modeling is essential in human computer vision; static single-frame predictions inevitably suffer from high false-alarm rates.
* Software engineering best practices (modular separation, automated unit tests, clean documentation) are critical to delivering production-grade AI systems.
